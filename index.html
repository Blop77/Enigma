    How to use the Enigma simulator

    1

    Agree on a key

    Both people choose the same Machine settings: rotors, ring settings, start positions, reflector and plugboard. Use 🎲 Random key, then 📋 Copy key and send it to your friend.

    2

    Encrypt

    Pick the 🔒 Encrypt tab, type your message and press Encrypt message. Copy the scrambled result and send it.

    3

    Decrypt

    Your friend loads the same key (📥 Load key), picks 🔓 Decrypt, pastes the ciphertext and presses Decrypt message. The original text appears!

    Two ways to use it

    • Like a real operator: click the round keys (or type on your keyboard). Each key lights a lamp — that's the encrypted letter. The rotors click forward after every key. Letters collect on the Tape.
    • The quick way: paste a whole message in the Message panel. Tick Animate on the machine to watch it being typed.

    Why decrypting works

    Enigma is reciprocal: if A becomes Q at some position, then Q becomes A at the same position. So decrypting is just encrypting the ciphertext again from the same start position. The simulator always starts a message from the start position set in Machine settings, so you never have to reset by hand.

    Parts of the machine

    Rotors (Walzen)
    Three wired wheels that scramble the alphabet. The right rotor steps on every key press; it pushes the middle one forward once per turn, which in turn pushes the left one. Turn them by hand with the ▲ ▼ buttons, the mouse wheel or arrow keys.
    Ring setting (Ringstellung)
    Shifts each rotor's internal wiring relative to its letters — another part of the secret key.
    Reflector (Umkehrwalze, UKW)
    Sends the current back through the rotors by a different route. This is why a letter can never encrypt to itself — the flaw that helped Alan Turing and the codebreakers at Bletchley Park crack Enigma.
    Plugboard (Steckerbrett)
    Cables that swap pairs of letters before and after the rotors. Historically 10 cables were used, adding about 150 trillion possibilities.

    Good to know

    • Only the 26 letters are encrypted. Digits and punctuation pass through unchanged (or are dropped in the authentic formats) — real operators spelled numbers out.
    • Operators wrote ciphertext in groups of five letters and used X for spaces. Choose those under Spaces & punctuation for the authentic look.
    • Keyboard shortcuts: letters A–Z press machine keys (when you're not typing in a text box), Esc cancels a half-made plug connection.
